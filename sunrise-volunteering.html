Bringing UHAF Home: A Day of Stories, Shared Futures, and Service at Sunrise of Issaquah

When we started the Universal Health Access Foundation (UHAF), our main focus was bridging gaps in healthcare. But after coordinating large-scale medical camps overseas in places like Barmer and Jodhpur, we wanted to find a direct, personal way to bring that same heart for service right back to our local community.

On Saturday, May 27th, part of our team had the incredible privilege of spending the afternoon with about 25 residents at the Sunrise of Issaquah Senior Center. We didn’t want to just show up and run through a generic volunteer routine; we wanted to truly engage with them.

A Shared Vision for the Future

When we originally reached out to the center to see what the seniors would be interested in, they surprised us by explicitly asking to hear a youth perspective. The four of us took turns giving a presentation on our thoughts regarding the current state of our country and our explicit goals as future leaders.

Standing up there and sharing our visions with them was a deeply grounding experience. The residents listened so closely and shared decades of their own life wisdom back with us. During our time there, we had the absolute honor of interviewing Mr. Howie. He shared incredible stories with us about his experience serving in the Navy, and he kindly told us that he thought our presentations were great. It reminded us that true leadership isn't just about looking forward—it’s about listening to the generations who built our present.

Connecting Over Games and Crafts

After the presentation, we spent the rest of the afternoon connecting on a very personal level. We facilitated a highly competitive cornhole tournament that brought out endless laughs, team spirit, and high-fives. Next, we sat down side-by-side with the residents to help them assemble and paint custom wooden birdhouse kits. The crafts were fun, but sharing stories about their families and lives in Issaquah over the craft tables was easily the highlight of the day.

Behind the Scenes

True service also means taking care of the small things. After the activities wrapped up, we noticed some glassware left over from previous resident activities. We rolled up our sleeves and handwashed all of it, making sure the hardworking facility staff had a clean slate for the upcoming week. No job is too small when it comes to supporting a community that welcomes you.

"

I was very impressed with Advaith, Siddharth, Nico, and Neelansh. Their professional knowledge and preparation to make the presentation interesting and appropriate for the seniors

— Ms. Reid, Activities Coordinator

This afternoon was an eye-opening reminder that service isn't just about high-level ideas—it’s about companionship, mutual respect, and rolling up your sleeves. We are incredibly excited to build on this momentum, expand our local volunteer team, and continue working closely with Sunrise of Issaquah throughout the summer and into the future!
